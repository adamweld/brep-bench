A cone with its tip rounded: the cone on the z axis with its base, of radius 6, at z = 0 and its
apex at z = 12, so that its side leans in 1 for every 2 it rises, and the tip rounded by a ball of
radius 1 tangent to the side. The ball's center is on the axis at z = 12 − √5, and it touches the
side along the circle of radius 2/√5 at z = 12 − 4/√5; above that circle the solid's surface is the
ball, below it the cone. Shrink it by 2: the solid of every point at least 2 from the solid's
outside.

The side, moved 2 inward, is the cone about the same axis with the same lean and its apex 2√5 lower,
at z = 12 − 2√5. The ball's face, moved 2 inward, would be a ball of radius 1 − 2, less than
nothing: it is no part of the result, and the moved side runs on to its own apex. The base moves up
to z = 2.

The result is a plain cone: its base, of radius h/2, at z = 2 and its apex h above, at z = 12 − 2√5,
with h = 10 − 2√5 ≈ 5.528.

**As a set:** every point with 2 ≤ z ≤ 12 − 2√5 and ρ ≤ (12 − 2√5 − z)/2, ρ its distance from the z
axis.
