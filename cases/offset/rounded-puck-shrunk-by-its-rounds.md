A puck: the cylinder of radius 6 on the z axis from z = 0 to z = 10 with both its circular edges
rounded to radius 2, each round a quarter of a torus whose tube has radius 2 about the circle of
radius 4 at z = 2 or z = 8. Shrink it by 2: the solid of every point at least 2 from the puck's
outside.

That is exactly the rounds' radius. Each round, moved 2 inward, shrinks onto its tube's center line,
the circle of radius 4 at z = 2 or z = 8: it has no area and is an edge of the result, where the
moved side, the cylinder of radius 4, meets the moved top or bottom at a right angle. The result is
the plain cylinder of radius 4 from z = 2 to z = 8, its edges sharp.

**As a set:** the puck is that cylinder grown by 2, every point within 2 of it, so shrinking it by 2
gives the cylinder back.
