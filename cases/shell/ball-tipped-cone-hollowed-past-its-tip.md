A cone with its tip rounded: the cone on the z axis with its base, of radius 6, at z = 0 and its
apex at z = 12, so that its side leans in 1 for every 2 it rises, and the tip rounded by a ball of
radius 1 tangent to the side. The ball's center is on the axis at z = 12 − √5, and it touches the
side along the circle of radius 2/√5 at z = 12 − 4/√5; above that circle the solid's surface is the
ball, below it the cone. Hollow it to a wall 1.5 thick with no opening: every face moves 1.5 inward
and the moved faces bound a closed cavity.

The side, moved 1.5 inward, is the cone about the same axis with the same lean and its apex 1.5√5
lower, at z = 12 − 1.5√5. The ball's face, moved 1.5 inward, would be a ball of radius 1 − 1.5, less
than nothing: it is no part of the result, and the moved side runs on to its own apex. The base
moves up to z = 1.5, the cavity's floor.

The cavity is a plain cone, its floor of radius h/2 at z = 1.5 and its apex at z = 12 − 1.5√5, with
h = 10.5 − 1.5√5 ≈ 7.146: the wall is 1.5 thick everywhere but over the tip, where it is thicker, 1
+ √5/2 along the axis.

**As a set:** the rounded cone less the open cone with its base at z = 1.5, radius h/2, and its apex
at z = 12 − 1.5√5.
