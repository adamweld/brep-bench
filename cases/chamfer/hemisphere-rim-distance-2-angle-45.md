The upper half of a ball of radius 5 centered on the origin, z ≥ 0. Chamfer the circle where its
flat face z = 0 meets the ball by a distance and an angle: 2 in from the rim on the flat face, the
chamfer making 45° with the flat face.

In the plane through the z axis the flat face is the straight line z = 0 out to the rim. The
chamfer starts on it at (3.0, 0), 2 in from the rim, and is the straight line from there that
rises at 45° toward the rim's side until it meets the ball, 2.406 along, at Q ≈ (4.702, 1.702).
The chamfer is the cone that line sweeps about the z axis, and the solid is the hemisphere cut by
it: the flat face shrinks to the disk of radius 3.0, and the ball keeps everything above Q.

The angle is measured from the flat face. On a ball the chamfer's two legs are not equal even at
45°: the line reaches the ball 1.702 above the flat, not 2.
